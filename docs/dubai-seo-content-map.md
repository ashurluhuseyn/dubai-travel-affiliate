# Caspaya Dubai SEO content map

**Market:** English-language visitors planning a leisure trip to Dubai  
**Research baseline:** 24 August 2026  
**Scope:** 30 future articles; the published `/blog/dubai-in-48-hours`
article is the seed page and is not counted in the 30.

## What this map is — and is not

This is an editorial and search-intent map, not a promise of rankings. It is
designed to prevent random publishing, duplicated search intent and articles
that cannot be kept accurate.

No search-volume or keyword-difficulty numbers are included because Caspaya is
not currently connected to Google Keyword Planner, Ahrefs or Semrush. Invented
metrics would create false confidence. Priorities below are based on:

- visible search-result patterns and recurring traveller questions;
- relevance to Caspaya's Dubai-first positioning;
- ability to answer the query with authoritative sources;
- usefulness for internal linking and future affiliate journeys;
- freshness risk and the work required to maintain accuracy.

Once Search Console has enough query and impression data, the map should be
re-prioritised using Caspaya's own evidence.

## Labels

- **Intent:** `Plan` means trip planning, `Learn` means informational,
  `Compare` means decision support and `Book-later` means commercial intent
  that may support affiliate offers only after real partners are connected.
- **Priority:** `P1` is the first publishing wave, `P2` follows after the first
  cluster is established and `P3` requires more authority, original evidence
  or frequent maintenance.
- **Refresh:** `Low` is mostly evergreen, `Medium` needs a scheduled review and
  `High` contains rules, prices, schedules or seasonal operations that must be
  checked shortly before publication and regularly afterward.

## First publishing wave: 12 articles

The order builds useful parent pages before narrower child pages. It also mixes
competitive pillars with specific, answerable queries instead of publishing
twelve broad “best things” lists.

1. **Dubai for First-Time Visitors** — foundation hub for the whole site.
2. **Best Time to Visit Dubai** — core planning decision and seasonal parent.
3. **Getting Around Dubai** — supports every itinerary and neighbourhood page.
4. **3 Days in Dubai** — natural next step after the existing 48-hour guide.
5. **Where to Stay in Dubai** — location choice has a direct effect on every route.
6. **Old Dubai on Foot** — specific, map-led and well supported by official sources.
7. **Free Things to Do in Dubai** — useful counterweight to Dubai's luxury image.
8. **Burj Khalifa Tickets and Levels** — strong decision intent; no affiliate link yet.
9. **Dubai Desert Safari Guide** — prepares future affiliate content responsibly.
10. **Dubai Beaches Compared** — visual and practical, with good Instagram reuse.
11. **5 Days in Dubai** — expands the itinerary cluster without duplicating 48 hours.
12. **Dubai with Kids** — a distinct audience guide with strong practical depth.

Articles 1–6 should be published first. Then Search Console impressions and
queries should be reviewed before fixing the exact order of 7–12.

## Cannibalisation rules

1. The first-time guide is a summary hub; it must not contain a full itinerary,
   full transport tutorial or full neighbourhood comparison.
2. The existing 48-hour article owns the exact two-day/48-hour intent. The
   three-day and five-day pages must have distinct routes and titles.
3. The best-time guide owns month comparison. Winter, summer and Ramadan pages
   answer season-specific planning questions and link back to it.
4. The where-to-stay guide compares all major visitor areas briefly. Marina
   versus Downtown owns the detailed two-area decision.
5. The desert-safari pillar explains the product and booking checks. Morning
   versus evening owns the format comparison.
6. “Old Dubai walking route” and “free things to do” may mention the same
   places, but only the former owns turn-by-turn route intent.

## Editorial quality gate for every article

A page remains `draft` and `noindex` until all of the following are true:

- one primary search intent and one clear reader outcome;
- a useful angle that is not merely a summary of competing pages;
- current authoritative sources with access dates;
- no invented personal visit, local-expert status, price, review or ranking;
- original route logic, comparison framework, checklist or analysis;
- accurate author/byline and a short explanation of how the article was made;
- descriptive cover-image alt text and, when possible, original Caspaya media;
- helpful internal links to published pages only;
- SEO title, meta description, canonical, Article schema and sitemap date;
- editorial review, lint, tests and production build.

High-refresh pages also need a visible “last reviewed” date and a calendar
reminder. A changed date without a substantive review is not acceptable.

## Source routing

Article research should begin with primary sources, then use reputable
secondary sources only to discover questions or add clearly attributed
context.

- [Visit Dubai](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/) — destination, itinerary,
  attraction and weather overview.
- [Dubai Roads and Transport Authority](https://www.rta.ae/) — Metro, tram,
  bus, nol, taxi and marine transport.
- [Dubai Airports](https://www.dubaiairports.ae/) — DXB terminal and passenger
  information.
- [UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae)
  — official visitor rules, culture and government service routing.
- [GDRFA Dubai](https://www.gdrfad.gov.ae/en) — Dubai entry and visa services.
- [Burj Khalifa](https://www.burjkhalifa.ae/) — official At the Top products
  and visitor instructions.
- Official attraction, museum, hotel or operator terms for product-specific
  facts; never copy aggregator prices as if they were permanent.

Google's current guidance says content should be created primarily for people,
clearly identify who made it and leave the reader with enough information to
achieve their goal. It also states that following best practices does not
guarantee crawling, indexing or rankings. See
[Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
and the [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide).

## Measurement plan

For each article, record a baseline on publication and review it after Google
has had time to process the page. Search changes can take weeks or longer, so
daily ranking reactions should not drive rewrites.

Track:

- indexing status and canonical selection in Search Console;
- impressions, clicks, click-through rate and average position by query;
- which unplanned queries generate impressions;
- internal-link clicks and engaged reading behaviour;
- source facts that need scheduled re-verification;
- affiliate clicks only after real affiliate offers are enabled.

After six researched articles are live, use actual Search Console queries to
decide whether the next article should follow this planned order or answer a
new opportunity already visible for Caspaya.
